Hi, I’m Ollie.

I travel solo with Type 1 diabetes and severe allergies, which means I pack more needles than socks and have asked far too many hostel receptionists how trustworthy their fridge is.

I’m 25, from the UK, and I document the honest version of travelling with conditions that make every journey slightly less straightforward. You’ll still see the mountains, motorbike loops, street food and people I meet. You’ll also see the insulin storage, allergy conversations, airport nerves, unexpected hypos and the occasional medical disaster.

This isn’t about pretending diabetes and allergies are nothing to worry about. It’s about showing what travel actually looks like when you prepare properly, things still go wrong and you find a way forward anyway.

A bit about me

I had wanted to travel the world since I was about twelve. Then COVID arrived, followed by a Type 1 diabetes diagnosis. I already lived with severe peanut and shellfish allergies. Diabetes added insulin storage, glucose sensors and nighttime hypos to the planning, but it did not end the ambition.

In early 2026, I left the UK and spent five months backpacking through Thailand, Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia, Indonesia and Malaysia. Some things went brilliantly. Others did not. My insulin was accidentally frozen in northern Vietnam, but I also completed motorbike loops, trekked through jungles, made friends from around the world and volunteered with Action4Diabetes in Cambodia.

I share the practical guides and honest mistakes I wanted before leaving, alongside stories from places that deserve more than a rushed photo stop.
