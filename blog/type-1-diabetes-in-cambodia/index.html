Type 1 Diabetes in Cambodia

By Ollie · Published 15 October 2026

Cambodia changed how I view my diabetes forever.

Since I set off on my solo adventures around Asia, I had become extremely curious about what Type 1 diabetes might be like for the people who live here.

I contacted Action4Diabetes before my trip, an amazing UK-registered charity which supports Type 1 peer support events and provides insulin and vital diabetes supplies across seven countries in Southeast Asia.

With the aim of making a few videos and spreading awareness of the cause, they suggested I attend a peer support event in Phnom Penh.

A day of peer support in Phnom Penh

Little did I know how bad the health inequalities were in Cambodia.

I met with the peer support organiser, and he explained what the day would entail.

I couldn’t help but notice the similar structure to UK events I had attended with Diabetes UK.

The day is less about the medical condition and more about the togetherness and unique solidarity that people with Type 1 naturally share with each other. I soon realised that these events may be even more vital here than in England.

What Sarin explained to me

Sarin explained the Type 1 diabetes situation in Cambodia to me. He told me that insulin and supplies were incredibly expensive and hard to access.

Heartbreakingly, he explained that some families have to choose between insulin and basic necessities like food. This can mean being unable to afford the insulin that keeps their child alive while also providing for the rest of their family.

This truly paralysed me.

My privilege as a British national hit me harder than ever. I vowed never to take the NHS for granted again.

My biggest worry was getting my five-month supply of insulin across borders. Some people cannot access insulin in the first place.

As I began to mingle with all the attendees at the event, this privilege became much more real.

“It was her dream to have a CGM”

I remember a girl the same age as me saw the sensor on my arm and said it was her dream to have a CGM.

How could I have taken my access to advanced technologies for granted for the last five years?

I ate dinner with these amazing people with Type 1 and realised we all share similar struggles, as all diabetics do. The difference is that here, they aren’t supported by an amazing free healthcare service like the NHS.

This is why I am a massive advocate for travelling with Type 1, as it really can change your perspective forever.

Ever since my time with A4D, I have vowed to spread awareness of the cause.

This is a strong reason why I decided on Cambodia as my first group-trip destination.
