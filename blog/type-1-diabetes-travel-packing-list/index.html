My Complete Type 1 Diabetes Travel Packing List

The categories I use when packing diabetes supplies, allergy equipment and general travel kit — because “I’ll remember it” is a bold strategy.

Published 31 July 2026 · Updated 31 July 2026 · 7 min read

Medical disclaimer: This is not medical advice. Use this as a planning prompt and confirm your own packing needs with your diabetes team, allergy specialist or healthcare professional.

Insulin

Pack enough insulin for your trip, plus backup supplies in case of delays, lost luggage or the general chaos that travel occasionally specialises in. Keep it protected from temperature extremes and follow the guidance from your healthcare team.

Pens or delivery equipment

Bring the delivery equipment you use every day, plus sensible backups. Editor note: add pump/cannula details only if Ollie wants to publish his exact setup.

Glucose monitoring equipment and spare sensors

Pack glucose monitoring equipment, chargers if needed, spare sensors and whatever backup method your diabetes team recommends. Don’t rely on one piece of tech behaving perfectly for an entire trip.

Needles and sharps storage

Carry enough needles or sharps supplies for your trip and plan how you will store used sharps safely until you can dispose of them properly.

Hypo treatments

Keep hypo treatments accessible, especially on buses, flights, hikes and days where meals may be delayed. The important word is accessible — not buried at the bottom of a backpack under three T-shirts and one questionable poncho.

Medical letters and prescriptions

Carry medical letters, prescriptions and any documentation needed for airport security, border crossings or replacing supplies if something goes wrong.

Insulin cooling equipment

I travelled with two extra-large FRIO wallets and one duo pen wallet. Cooling equipment is a backup tool, not a guarantee, so learn how it works before leaving.

Backup supplies

Split supplies between bags where possible. Losing one bag should not mean losing absolutely everything important.

Severe-allergy equipment

I travel with severe-allergy equipment, including two adrenaline auto-injectors and food-allergy translation cards. Check expiry dates, storage guidance and airline/security rules before you go.

General travel essentials

Think travel insurance, phone power, universal adaptor, copies of documents, packing organisers and a system that makes it easy to find supplies when tired.
